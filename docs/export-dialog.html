Groove · EXP-004 design study · #724 · chosen: B · Release

Export Dialog Studies

Three takes on the Export dialog, built from the faceplate parts: the switch, the group rule, label-over-value readouts, pressed toggles and one white action. Each one is a working prototype. Press Export, cancel partway, force a failure, or load the 52-track project to hit the 2 GB limit and turn tracks off until it fits.

A · Console

The #724 spec, built exactly. The format is a two-option switch. The tracks are a tall, filterable list of pressed toggles in track order, returns last. The footer holds the readouts: size against the 2 GB limit, then progress. Nothing moves when you switch format. In stereo the list stays in place but locks, because the mix is what you hear.

  • Progress is shown per stem: a hairline in the track's colour fills under its row, so a long render shows where it is.
  • Over budget, Export stays focusable with aria-disabled. The alert names the limit and how far over the selection is.
  • Package lists every file in the ZIP, so the producer sees the 01 BD.wav names before they commit.

B · Release

Exporting means the song is done, so this dialog leads with the song. Its name is the title, and the whole arrangement is drawn as coloured lanes. The track list is the lanes' own name column, so the list stays vertical and each toggle sits beside the music it controls. Turn a track off and its lane goes dark.

  • While it renders, a white playhead crosses the song and prints each lane at full brightness behind it.
  • When it's done, the lanes gather into a sleeve drawn only in the tracks' own colours. The chrome stays monochrome. Next to the sleeve: the song's stats and what to do next.
  • The format choice uses the producer's words: "Share a mix" or "Stems for hand off".
  • Picking tracks. Click flips one track. ⇧-click sets every track between it and the last one you clicked to match that one. ⌘/Ctrl-click picks tracks without flipping them; then On, Off or "Only these" act on the picked tracks, or click any one of them to flip them all. Clicking a lane in the arrangement does the same as clicking its name. From the keyboard: arrows, ⇧+arrows to pick a run, Space to flip, ⌘A to pick all.
  • Always in batches. Stems never hit a size block. Up to 2 GB they come as one ZIP. Over 2 GB they split in track order into ZIPs that each fit under the limit, and a note under the footer says so. The split shows as numbered brackets beside the lanes and as cards in the Downloads row. Each ZIP downloads when it is done, before the next one starts. Cancel or a failure keeps the finished ZIPs and offers "Resume from ZIP 2". Flag This changes #66, which blocks Export over 2 GB. It needs a product decision and an amendment to the issue.
  • No layout shift. The Downloads row, the readouts, the buttons and the one-line message slot keep fixed sizes, so a message, a failure or a label change never moves anything.

C · Print matrix Exploration

Borrowed from Teenage Engineering: a device, not a form. It has a dot-matrix display with tape reels, square numbered keys with small LEDs, and tiny lowercase mono labels. There is no modal and no format switch. Export becomes a print unit that slides up over the arrangement, like a rack unit at the end of the chain. Tracks are rows and output files are columns. A white pad means "this track goes into that file". The files it will write are listed along the bottom and update as you press pads.

  • Mix is the stereo WAV. Stem is one WAV per track. + Bus adds a grouped stem, such as all drums in one file. Grouped stems go beyond #66's scope.
  • Printing works like recording to tape. The reels on the display spin and the take-up reel fills, each row's meter moves in its track colour, and each file card fills as it renders.
  • A pressed key is white, with a small LED in the track's colour. The budget is a 20-segment bar, and the last segment hatches when you go over.
  • The 2 GB budget counts files, so grouping tracks into a bus is another way to get under it.
