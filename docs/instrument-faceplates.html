Instrument Faceplates

Five Post-Alpha instruments in the Faceplate System. Every faceplate follows the same layout, so a user who knows one knows them all. Drag a fader up or down (hold Shift for fine moves), use the arrow keys, or double-click to reset. The wells redraw as you go. Choose a preset or roll the die. There is no sound in this study.

  • HeaderTrack and a few readouts on the left. On the right: the meter, Randomize on the synths, Preset: and its name, then Audition. "edited" appears once you change anything.
  • NamesOne word set everywhere: Osc 1 and Osc 2 (Oscillator when there is one), Filter, Voice, Amp / Filter / Mod envelope, LFO, Res, Cutoff, Low cut.
  • OrderSections follow the signal: oscillators, filter, voice, envelopes (amp first), LFOs, then modulation.
  • BandA row of sections. Each section stacks the same four rows: heading, well, switch, faders. Headings, wells, switches and faders each line up across the whole band.
  • Fader pitchA section's width follows its number of faders, so faders keep one spacing from group to group.
  • WellOne height in every band. It draws what the section does; a section with nothing to draw shows its facts in the same black panel instead.
  • Switch rowPresent in a band when any of its sections has a switch. Sections without one leave the row empty, so their faders still line up.
  • RandomizeA die. One per synth in its header, left of the preset, and one per voice on the drum synth's rows. Level and pan are never randomized.

Synth

#940: Osc 1 mixes saw, pulse, sub and noise, the way the SH-101 and Juno do it, and Osc 2 adds a detuned layer. Then a multi-mode filter, voice controls (unison and glide) and two envelopes. #939's LFO sits inside the instrument, with one depth per destination.

  • Roland S-1 and SH-101: the mixer and the LFO depths
  • Juno-60: the high-pass filter
Kept
Mixed waveforms instead of a waveform switch; one LFO with three depths, as on the S-1; two envelopes; unison and glide.
Changed
Modern names only: Octave not Range, Res not Emphasis, Env not Contour, Low cut not HPF, Voices not Unison.
Watch
Osc 2 is the one part the 101 and S-1 do not have. It earns its place by doubling the oscillator for leads and pads.

Wavetable

#944: two wavetable oscillators with position and one warp, one filter, voice controls, two envelopes and two LFOs. Modulation stays inside the instrument: the mod envelope and each LFO pick one destination from a list.

  • Serum and Vital: oscillator rows
  • Ableton Wavetable: restraint
Kept
Position is the hero control. The table picker lives in the well it changes.
Cut
Wavetable editor, sample and noise oscillators, spectral warps, the FX rack, a mod matrix page.
Watch
One destination per source keeps routing readable. A second destination per source is the first thing users will ask for.

FM

#945: a six-operator, 32-algorithm DX7-compatible engine behind a small Volca-style panel. The four envelope faders offset the loaded patch; the operator table is the detail.

  • Korg Volca FM: the macro faders
  • Yamaha DX7: the algorithm chart
  • Dexed: the operator table
Kept
Algorithm, feedback, the four attack and decay offsets, LFO, transpose and velocity, and each operator's ratio and level.
Cut
Per-operator envelopes, key scaling and pitch envelope from the face. They load with the patch.
Watch
The study steps through 9 of the 32 algorithms. All 32 need a compact chart.

Drum synth

#989: every voice is the same small engine: an oscillator with a pitch drop, filtered noise, mixed, clicked, decayed and driven. The table follows the drum machine's; the selected voice opens in place.

  • Microtonic: the per-voice engine
  • TR-808 / 909: tune, snap, decay
Kept
Four faders per row (tune, snap, decay, level) with the full engine one click away. A die on every row.
Cut
Microtonic's pattern morphing and per-voice EQ; effects are devices.
Watch
Snap is the oscillator–noise mix. It reads as "snappy" on a snare and as body on a kick; the name has to cover both.

Sampler

#122 and #943 on one instrument. The sample runs the full width. Playback chooses Whole (a start–end window, pitched across the keys) or Sliced (each key plays one slice). Slices holds both the slicing settings and the selected slice's pitch and gain. Transpose, fine tune, the filter and the hold stage apply in both modes.

  • Ableton Simpler: Classic and Slice modes
  • MPC: slicing by transient, grid or hand
Kept
#122's fine tune, filter and hold stage in both modes. #943's three ways to slice, with per-slice pitch and gain.
Cut
Sampler gain and pan, and per-slice pan: the mixer already has them. Pads: the piano roll and step grid play the slices.
Watch
Per-slice gain stays because it balances slices against each other, which the track fader cannot.
